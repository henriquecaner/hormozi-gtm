## Chapter 2 — Grand Slam Offers (essence)

A Grand Slam Offer combines **pricing, value, guarantees, and naming** so that the transaction feels lopsided in the customer's favor.

Three levers:
1. Maximize the Value Equation
2. Solve every perceived problem on the path to the result
3. Position in the right market (high pain + buying power + no dominant solution)

## Chapter 6 — Value Equation

```
Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Probability of Success) / (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice)
```

Four vectors:

- **Dream Outcome** — what the customer ACTUALLY wants (not the product)
- **Perceived Probability of Success** — the belief that it will work FOR THEM
- **Time Delay** — time until a measurable result
- **Effort & Sacrifice** — what the customer has to DO and GIVE UP

Increase the numerator, shrink the denominator. Always.

## Chapters 11–13 — Bonus Stacking

Bonus-stacking rules:
- Odd quantity (3, 5, 7)
- Each bonus with its own name + dollar value + clear deliverable
- Total bonus value ≥ 3–5x the price charged
- The most valuable perceived bonus sits at #2 or #3, not #1
- Trim & Stack: pull from the core and move it to a bonus when it makes sense

## Chapter 14 — Scarcity & Urgency

Hormozi distinguishes genuine from fake:

**Genuine** (use it):
- Limited spots based on real capacity
- Limited cohort with a date
- Fast-action bonus tied to a time window
- A real price increase

**Fake** (don't use it):
- A countdown that resets
- "Last spot" that's permanent
- Manufactured urgency with no reason behind it

Genuine scarcity = actions you'd take even if no one knew about them.

## Chapter 15 — Guarantees (Risk Reversal)

4 types:
1. **Money-back** — unconditional, simple
2. **Results-based** — conditional, measurable
3. **Time-based** — trial period
4. **Performance** — compensation beyond "money back"

A generic guarantee ("satisfaction guaranteed") = no guarantee.

A good guarantee has 5 elements:
- An actionable condition (not passive)
- A measurable metric ($, deals, %, time)
- A clear timeframe
- Concrete compensation
- A simple claim process

## Naming (cross-chapters)

Naming moves conversion on its own. Principles:
- Promise a benefit, not a feature
- Specific > generic
- Memorable (rhyme, alliteration, a good acronym)
- A named mechanism beats "my approach"
