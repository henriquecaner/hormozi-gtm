# Founder-Market Fit

Source: Alex Hormozi, *$100M Leads* (Ch. 1, "Why People Listen") + journal extracts ("Truth tellers") + LEVEL reference.

## Why this skill exists

Hormozi says it over and over: "Don't start a business in a niche where you have no native credibility." Founder-market fit is the invisible prerequisite under everything:

- Without it, **a conditional guarantee reads like a bluff** (the client doesn't believe you can deliver).
- Without it, **a named mechanism reads like theory** (the client doesn't believe it ever worked for anyone).
- Without it, **a case study reads like a staged performance** (the client doubts you actually did it).
- Without it, **high pricing reads like nerve** (the client sees no reason to pay a premium).

A common mistake runs the other way: "I'll learn copywriting so I can sell a copywriting course." It works for a small niche for 6 months, then stalls — because authority you built artificially doesn't scale.

## The 3 types of founder-market fit

You fit at least one of these. Identify which before you pick a niche.

### Type 1: Native Expert

Worked in the niche **doing the problem** you now solve, for 5+ years.

**Examples:**
- Ex-Head of Sales at a B2B SaaS → sales consulting for B2B SaaS.
- Ex-CFO of a privately held company → M&A consulting for similar companies.
- Ex-CMO who scaled from $1M to $50M ARR → growth consulting for founders on the same curve.

**Strength:**
- Instant credibility; the ICP speaks your language.
- Career wins become consulting case studies (with permission).
- Premium pricing ($30k–200k per engagement) accessible from day 1.

**Risk:**
- Tendency to become "the consultant who only knows what he lived" — doesn't generalize the method.

**Fits niches:** high technical specialization, B2B enterprise, C-level decisions. Pricing > $50k/engagement is fine.

### Type 2: Customer-Turned-Coach

Was a customer of the problem, solved it, and now sells the path you walked. Not an expert by career — an expert by documented personal experience.

**Examples:**
- Lost 65 lbs in 8 months → weight-loss coach (clients hit the same result in 8–12 months).
- Went from $5k to $200k in revenue as a solo consultant → teaches solopreneurs.
- Pulled off a startup's first successful pivot → sells a pivoting framework to other founders.

**Strength:**
- The story sells itself.
- The ICP sees themselves in you ("he was me a year ago").
- Accessible to a less sophisticated audience (B2C, early-stage founders, people in career transition).

**Risk:**
- Lower pricing ceiling ($2k–30k typical). You scale through volume, not deal size.
- Hard to hold authority once a student outgrows the "teacher."

**Fits niches:** B2C, B2B small business, personal transformations (health, finance, career), founders in transition. Pricing $1k–30k.

### Type 3: Researcher-Learner

Neither an expert nor a former customer. Studied the problem deeply, interviewed 50+ people in the niche, and synthesized a method that works — without having lived it personally.

**Examples:**
- A journalist who studied 100 venture funds and wrote the fundraising framework.
- An academic who researched SaaS retention for 3 years without ever working in SaaS.
- A strategy consultant who interviewed 80 founders to build a playbook (without having founded anything himself).

**Strength:**
- Systemic view that operators don't have.
- Impartiality (doesn't defend one school — synthesizes several).
- A highly specialized B2B audience accepts this profile.

**Risk:**
- Fragile credibility until the first validated case.
- B2C / early-stage founder audiences tend to reject it ("you've never done it").
- Starting pricing is capped, then rises once the first 10–20 cases become proof.

**Fits niches:** senior B2B consulting, defensible proprietary frameworks, technical publications. Pricing starts low ($5k–20k) and grows as cases accumulate.

## How to identify your type

Ask yourself honestly:

| Question | Native Expert | Customer-Turned-Coach | Researcher-Learner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Years in the niche actually doing it? | 5+ | 0 (customer) | 0 |
| Documentable personal result? | Yes, several | Yes, 1 big one | No, synthesizes others' |
| Network in the niche? | 50+ C-level contacts | 50+ peer contacts | 50+ interviewees |
| Defensible starting pricing? | $30k+ | $2k–15k | $5k–15k |
| Time to first validated case? | 30–60 days | immediate (your own) | 90–180 days |

If you answer "no" to all 4 in every column → **you don't have founder-market fit yet.** Build it before you scale.

## How to build founder-market fit when you don't have it

**Step 1 — Pick a route:**

- **Route A (Native Expert):** work 18–24 months in the niche as a senior employee before going out on your own. Slower, but it builds a real network and real credibility.
- **Route B (Customer-Turned-Coach):** become a customer of the problem. Document the journey in public (1 post/week). Once you get the result, sell the path. 6–12 months.
- **Route C (Researcher-Learner):** interview 50+ people in the niche over 60–90 days. Publish the synthesis in a consumable format (newsletter, podcast, book). Become a reference through curation, not execution.

**Step 2 — Document the journey in public.**
Whatever the route, show weekly progress. Clients buy the journey, not the destination.

**Step 3 — First 5 cases free / very cheap.**
Take low pay on the first 5 to capture documented cases. Without external proof, founder-market fit is just a claim you're making.

**Step 4 — Raise pricing as proof accumulates.**
Each documented case = +20–40% on pricing. 5 cases = 3–5x your starting price.

## Common traps

**1. Skipping the fit stage and going straight to scale.**
A $5k course sold by someone with no fit converts 10–20% on the first batch (curiosity). Repeat purchase drops below 1% on the second. The audience figures it out.

**2. Inflating fit ("I consulted for Coca-Cola" when you didn't).**
A specific lie gets caught within 30 days by a B2B audience. It ends your career in that niche.

**3. Thinking that consuming the niche's content = fit.**
Reading 50 books on pricing isn't fit. It's just a baseline. Fit needs documented execution or a rigorous synthesis of real interviews.

**4. Switching niches without rebuilding fit.**
A founder with fit in "B2B SaaS sales" doesn't automatically have fit in "sales to medical clinics." The proof resets.

**5. Confusing audience with fit.**
Having 100k LinkedIn followers is audience, not fit. Audience can be built on entertainment or controversy — fit is the credibility to solve the specific problem.

## When this skill kicks in

| Case | How to use it |
|---|---|
| `/hormozi-gtm:init` | First question before mapping the ICP: "What's your fit in this niche? (1, 2, 3, or none)" |
| Offer audit with low Probability | A Probability of 4–5/10 is often missing fit, not missing copy. Suggest building fit before touching copy. |
| `/hormozi-gtm:plan` early (pre-PMF) | Include a "first 5 documented cases" milestone in Q1. |

## When it does NOT kick in

- Founder already has 20+ documented cases, NPS > 50, healthy repeat business → fit is built, focus on scaling.
- Refining copy for a product that already sells well → use `grand-slam-offer` directly.
