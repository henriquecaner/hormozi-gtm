# Productization

Source: Alex Hormozi, *$100M Money Models* (videos 10, 17, 22) + cross-reference with `pricing-playbook` (Law 4, Tiering) and `money-models`.

## Why this skill exists

Once your 1:1 service works, the instant temptation is "I'll turn it into a SaaS / course / program." That jump usually breaks unit economics because you package before the method is stable and before the audience is ready to self-serve.

Other skills in the plugin (`money-models`, `pricing-playbook`) assume the product format is already set. This one covers the decision that comes **before**: what format you have today, what the next one is, how to make the transition without losing revenue or diluting delivery.

## The 5 formats (highest deal size to lowest)

| Format | Typical deal size | Margin | Effort per client | Scalability |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1:1 consulting/coaching** | $30k-200k+ | 60-80% | High | Low (capped by your hours) |
| **Group / Mastermind** | $5k-50k | 70-90% | Medium (cohort sync) | Medium |
| **Live cohort course** | $2k-15k | 80-95% | Medium (live calls + community) | Medium-high |
| **Self-paced digital course** | $297-3k | 90-98% | Low (produce once, distribute forever) | High |
| **SaaS / software product** | $99-5k/mo | 70-85% | Minimal (support) | Very high |

**Key trade-off:** the more scalable, the more commoditized and the more competitive pressure.

## When to move from one format to the next

Every transition has a quantitative and a qualitative **gate**. Jump without the gate and you break economics or dilute quality.

### 1:1 → Group / Mastermind

**Quantitative gate:**
- ≥ 20 1:1 clients served with the same problem/method.
- LTV:CAC > 4:1 on the 1:1 (enough margin to absorb efficiency loss in the group).
- Wait time for new clients ≥ 30 days (demand exceeds capacity).

**Qualitative gate:**
- You can articulate the method as 5-7 repeatable frameworks (not just "feel").
- 1:1 clients ask for community or peer learning.

**Pivot structure:**
- Keep 1:1 as the high-ticket tier for 6-12 months.
- Launch a group at 50-60% of the 1:1 price with 4-8 people.
- Run 2 cohorts before retiring 1:1 (if you retire it at all).

### Group → Live cohort course

**Quantitative gate:**
- ≥ 3 group cohorts run, NPS > 50.
- Completion rate ≥ 60% (clients finish the program).
- Group revenue per cohort > $50k (proves recurring demand).

**Qualitative gate:**
- Content is already documented (slides, frameworks, materials).
- You can teach 20-50 people at once (no longer 4-8).

**Structure:**
- 4-8 week cohort with weekly live calls + async community.
- Mastermind bonus (limited 1:1 access) becomes a paid upsell.

### Cohort course → Self-paced digital course

**Quantitative gate:**
- ≥ 5 live cohorts run with the same curriculum.
- Completion rate ≥ 50% (the curriculum holds up on its own).
- ≥ 100 cumulative paying clients (proof + cases).

**Qualitative gate:**
- 80% of the questions during live calls repeat (a FAQ exists).
- The async community runs without you present.

**Structure:**
- Record all the content professionally.
- Keep the live cohort as the premium tier (3-5x the self-paced price).
- Self-paced becomes the tripwire / volume play.

### Course → SaaS

**Quantitative gate:**
- ≥ 500 course clients using the method.
- At least 30% of them ask to "automate this."
- You have capital for 12-18 months of development.

**Qualitative gate:**
- There's a clear process software can replicate (don't build a SaaS to teach thinking).
- You or your team has the technical chops (or capital to hire a CTO).

**⚠️ Warning:** this transition is the most expensive and the most often gotten wrong. Most consultants who become "SaaS" should have stayed in course + community.

## Pricing adjustment per transition

When you move formats, do **not** just divide the previous price. Each format has its own economics:

| From → To | Typical pricing ratio |
|---|---|
| 1:1 $50k → Group | Group: $15k-25k (30-50% of 1:1) |
| Group $20k → Cohort | Cohort: $5k-10k (25-50% of Group) |
| Cohort $8k → Self-paced course | Self-paced: $1k-3k (15-40% of Cohort) |
| Self-paced $2k → SaaS | SaaS: $199-499/mo (depends on retention) |

**Rule:** the new price must carry ≥ 70% margin AND an LTV:CAC ratio ≥ 3:1. If it doesn't, the format isn't ready.

## Validation before packaging (without building)

Before you spend 3-6 months building, validate demand:

**Pre-sell the next format:**
- Waitlist with a $100-300 deposit (refundable).
- Survey current clients: "If [next format] cost $X, would you buy it?"
- Fake door: a "Get notified" button + a validation funnel.

**Gate:** ≥ 30 people show explicit interest (deposit or a 9-10/10 survey response) before you build.

**Format MVP:**
- Group: run 1 pilot cohort with 4 people at 50% of the final price.
- Cohort: run 1 pilot cohort with 10-15 people, 4 weeks, validation pricing.
- Self-paced: record only the 3 core modules, sell at $297 as a "beta," collect 50 cases before finalizing.
- SaaS: a no-code prototype (Bubble, Webflow, Airtable) with 5-10 beta users for 60 days.

## Signals you packaged too early

- Conversion on the new format < 1% (the audience doesn't buy it).
- Completion rate < 30% (clients buy and abandon).
- NPS < 30 (quality dropped vs the previous format).
- Lots of refunds (clients expected something different).

**Fix:** back up a format. Don't stack; retreat. Stay in the previous format another 6-12 months, refine the method, try again.

## Signals you packaged too late

- 1:1 has a waitlist ≥ 90 days.
- You charge 5x the market and still have demand.
- 1:1 clients start teaching each other (proof the method can go 1:many).
- You're exhausted and can't serve clients + grow at the same time.

**Fix:** transition in parallel. Keep 1:1 as the premium tier, launch the next format within 90 days.

## Application by use case

| Case | How to use Productization |
|---|---|
| `/hormozi-gtm:plan` on a 1:1 founder at plateau | Include the analysis: are you in the right format? Does the next format make sense in the next 12 months? |
| `/hormozi-gtm:audit` with LTV:CAC tightening | Could be format saturation (1:1 capped by hours) — suggest group/cohort as a pressure release. |
| `/hormozi-gtm:pricing` mid format transition | The new format's pricing has to follow the ratio table (don't just divide the previous price). |

## When it does NOT apply

- Pre-PMF founder (doesn't have 20+ cases to justify packaging).
- Refining a single offer within the same format → use `grand-slam-offer`.
- The "which acquisition channel do I use" decision → use `core-four` or `leads-strategist`.
